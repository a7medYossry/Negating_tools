A Comprehensive Guide to Negation in Arabic

In Arabic, the word you use for "not" is very precise. It changes based on the tense (past, present, future) and the strength of the negation. This guide covers all the tools you need.

Part 1: Negating the Past Tense ("Did Not")

Tool 1: مَا — The Simple "Did Not"

This is the most direct way to negate a past event. Simply place مَا before the past tense verb. It does not change the verb.

He wrote: كَتَبَ

He did not write: مَا كَتَبَ

Tool 2: لَمْ — The Emphatic "Did Not"

This tool is very common and more emphatic than مَا. It uses a present tense verb to describe a past event. It also shortens the end of the verb (a grammatical mood called Jussive).

He writes: يَكْتُبُ

He did not write: لَمْ يَكْتُبْ

Part 2: Negating the Present Tense ("Do/Does Not")

Tool 1: لَا — The Standard "Don't / Doesn't"

Use لَا to negate actions in the present tense (habits or things happening now). It does not change the verb's ending.

I drink coffee: أَشْرَبُ القَهْوَة

I don't drink coffee: لَا أَشْرَبُ القَهْوَة

Tool 2: لَمَّا — The "Not Yet"

This is an advanced tool. لَمَّا negates something that started in the past and is still not done, but is expected to happen. It's the perfect equivalent of "not yet". Like لَمْ, it shortens the verb ending.

The train has arrived, and my brother has not yet arrived.

وَصَلَ القِطَارُ وَلَمَّا يَصِلْ أَخِي

Part 3: Negating the Future Tense ("Will Not")

Tool 1: لَنْ — The Definitive "Won't"

This is the strongest and clearest way to negate the future. It means "will not" and is very definitive. It changes the verb's final vowel sound from "u" to "a" (a mood called Subjunctive).

He will travel: سَيُسَافِرُ

He will not travel: لَنْ يُسَافِرَ

A Note on Using لَا for the Future

You can use لَا to negate the future, but it's less emphatic than لَنْ and requires a time word (like "tomorrow").

I will not go to the market tomorrow. (Less emphatic)

أَنَا لَا أَذْهَبُ إِلَى السُّوقِ غَدًا

Part 4: Special Cases & Advanced Negation

Tool 1: لَيْسَ — Negating "Being" (Is Not / Are Not)

لَيْسَ is a special verb used to negate identity, states, and descriptions—not actions. It functions like "is not" or "am not". The adjective that follows it gets an "-an" ending.

The teacher is new: المُدَرِّسُ جَدِيدٌ

The teacher is not new: لَيْسَ المُدَرِّسُ جَدِيدًا

Conjugations of لَيْسَ

  • I am not: لَسْتُ
  • You (m) are not: لَسْتَ
  • You (f) are not: لَسْتِ
  • He is not: لَيْسَ
  • She is not: لَيْسَتْ
  • We are not: لَسْنَا

Emphasizing with لَيْسَ

To make the negation stronger, you can add the letter بِـ to the adjective. This is very common in high-level Arabic.

Normal: The student is not absent. لَيْسَ الطَّالِبُ غَائِبًا

Emphatic: The student is not at all absent. لَيْسَ الطَّالِبُ بِغَائِبٍ

Tool 2: Negating a Command (Prohibition with لَا)

To tell someone "Don't do something!", you use a special form of لَا called لَا النَّاهِيَة (The Prohibiting Lā). It looks similar to the normal negating لَا, but it shortens the verb ending, just like لَمْ.

Negation vs. Prohibition: A Key Difference

Negation لَا النَّافِيَة: Describes what is not happening. (The verb stays the same)

He does not write: هُوَ لَا يَكْتُبُ


Prohibition لَا النَّاهِيَة: Gives a command not to do something. (The verb ending is shortened)

Don't write!: لَا تَكْتُبْ
